## 1. Credits

**Finding: I could not find a named staff list (director, designers, programmers, graphics, sound, producer) for the 1989 NES game in any source.**

- MobyGames' NES entry, archived January 2023, says verbatim "There are no game credits on file". It lists Developer: SNK Corporation and Publisher: SNK Corporation of America, released Jul 1989.
  http://web.archive.org/web/20230125033030/https://www.mobygames.com/game/nes/baseball-stars
  (The live MobyGames page returned HTTP 403, so I could not check whether credits were added after 2023.)
- The English Wikipedia infobox has an empty `designer` field and names only SNK as developer: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_Stars
- The Japanese catalogue gavas.jp (Pico Pico Daihyakka) lists no staff, only "© 1989 SNK": https://www.gavas.jp/products/detail.php?product_id=4929
- The US manual contains no staff credits. Its only corporate attribution is "SNK CORPORATION OF AMERICA, 246 SOBRANTE WAY, SUNNYVALE, CALIFORNIA 94086, © 1989 SNK CORPORATION OF AMERICA, PRINTED IN JAPAN". [Manual]
- **In-game credits roll:** UNSOURCED.
  No source I found documents a staff roll.
  TCRF and GameFAQs describe the ending only as a trophy screen, plus "a special celebratory image after getting the trophy" when you win with a custom or hidden team. [TCRF] [GF Cheats]
- **Not to be confused with the sequels.** Wikipedia lists staff for the sequels only, and these must not be attributed to the 1989 NES game:
  - *Baseball Stars Professional* (Neo Geo, 1990): designers Miki T. and Toyohisa Tanabe, composer Toshikazu Tanaka. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_Stars_Professional
  - *Baseball Stars 2* (Neo Geo, 1992): producer Eikichi Kawasaki, designer Masami Tokusue, composers Kazuhiro Nishida and Masahiko Hataya. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_Stars_2
- **Suggestion for the homage section:** credit "SNK" and "SNK Corporation of America" as the manual does, and dedicate the page to the SNK Crushers, the team the developers named after themselves (see §2).

## 2. SNK Crushers roster: real people?

**What is sourced: secondary sources say the Crushers are named after the game's developers. No source maps any individual name to a specific person.**

- NESJunk review by "BitRot" (2020, updated 2023): "with the players named after the developers of the game, they're a team that sucks in every regard!"
  https://nesjunk.com/2020/06/03/baseball-stars-1989-review-snk-hits-one-out-of-the-park/
- SABR Baseball Gaming, Bryan Walko, "Still Shining: Baseball Stars for the NES" (12 Feb 2023): the Crushers are "A team named after the developers at SNK."
  https://sabrbaseballgaming.com/2023/02/12/still-shining-baseball-stars-for-the-nes/
- Baseball Prospectus, "Short Relief" (9 Feb 2018; Crushers section by Matt Sussman) is a humorous piece about "the last-place SNK Crushers". It says only "They had Shacho." and offers no explanation of the name.
  https://www.baseballprospectus.com/news/article/37690/short-relief-shacho-aristotle-sutter-great-baseballers-antiquity/
- **"SHACHO":** 社長 (shachō) is the Japanese word for "company president; manager; director" (Jisho dictionary): https://jisho.org/search/shachou
  - That is a linguistic fact only.
  - **No source I found says SHACHO represents SNK founder and president Eikichi Kawasaki.** That identification is **UNSOURCED** and should be presented, if at all, as a plausible reading rather than a fact.
  - Wikipedia's only link between Kawasaki and the series is his producer credit on *Baseball Stars 2*: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_Stars_2
- **NOBE, IKAWA, HAMA, KOHNO, VINCE, MANNY, JAB, PAUL, NEAL, KJB, JUN, TOMMY, ED, TARO, GLEN, GEOFF, TYRONE:** UNSOURCED. I found no source tying any of them to a named person.
- **Supporting detail from the sequel.** The roster carries over to *Baseball Stars Professional* (Neo Geo, 1990).
  - The SNK fandom wiki's Crushers table (archived Sept 2025) lists these English names: Vince, Ikawa, Manny, Johnny, Neal, Kev, Shacho, Tommy, Jun, Hama, Nobe, Kohno, Ed, Taro, Glen, Frank.
  - 13 of these overlap with the NES list. Johnny, Kev and Frank are new; JAB, PAUL, KJB, GEOFF and TYRONE are absent.
  - In the Japanese version of Pro the team is "エスエヌケイバタリアンズ" (SNK Battalions), and the slots are filled by SNK game characters instead. For example, Shacho maps to クラーク (Clark), Jun to アテナ (Athena), and Ikawa to テリー (Terry).
  - Sources: http://web.archive.org/web/20250917114542/https://snk.fandom.com/wiki/Baseball_Stars_Professional and Japanese Wikipedia "ベースボールスターズ" (which calls the team "SNK版ナムコスターズ", SNK's version of Namco Stars, and the weakest team): https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%83%99%E3%83%BC%E3%82%B9%E3%83%9C%E3%83%BC%E3%83%AB%E3%82%B9%E3%82%BF%E3%83%BC%E3%82%BA
  - Swapping in-house names for SNK mascots in Japan is consistent with the English names being staff in-jokes, but no source states this outright. **UNSOURCED** inference.

## 3. Player growth, money and salaries

From the manual [Manual], quoted or closely paraphrased:

- **Earnings.** "You will earn money for your team if:"
  1. "You win in a 1 player vs. computer game."
  2. "You win in a 1 player game vs. any original team (computer controlled)."
  3. "You win a 1 player vs. 2 player game in league mode."

  "You will not win money in VS. mode if you are playing a 1 player vs. 2 player game."
- **Prestige** (a fielder ability) "affects audience and income numbers."
  Wikipedia adds that the purse depends on "the sum of the *prestige* ratings of the players from both teams (as prestige determines how many paying fans attend the game)". [WP]
- **Increasing Points (power-up), verbatim:**
  > "The dollar amount next to the player's face is the amount needed to raise that particular player's ability scores. (If you don't have the necessary funds this option will not appear.) Once you choose (A BUTTON) to pay the money to raise a player's scores, a random number between 1 and 6 will appear. This is the number of times that you can raise a score for the money paid. (You can raise one or more scores each time you power up, they don't have to go all into the same category.) … (You must use all the bonus points to continue.)"
- **Max Points:** "This is the number you may not exceed when tailoring your team players ability scores." [Manual]
- **Types of player you can hire:** [Manual]
  - ROOKIE: "ability points are low but his max ability points are high … a good choice for long term investment."
  - STAR PLAYER: "ability scores are already near a high maximum. His price is the highest of all the players."
  - VETERAN: "Most veterans are near their maximum ability score or at it … a fairly large price."
- **Roster rules.** No more than 5 pitchers and 13 fielders, 18 in total. You must fire a player before hiring one. A fired player's "stats will be set to zero". [Manual]
- **Trading.** Trades are allowed only between created teams. "If there is a significant difference between two different players being traded, the stronger player's stats will be changed so that they are close to that of the other player traded." [Manual]
- **Abilities:** [Manual]
  - Fielders: Hitting, Batting (power), Running, Defense, Chance, Prestige.
  - Pitchers: Stamina, Speed, and the curve and drop abilities ("Each point added will add one degree to the ball's curve").
  - The pitcher also has a second "SEE FIELDING ABILITY" screen.
- **Rising power-up cost and save-scumming.** A GameFAQs tip (by "Macolio") says "the cost of power-ups grows exponantially [sic]". Because the game autosaves, resetting *before* spending the points keeps your money, so you can re-roll for 6 points. [GF Cheats]
  The exact growth curve is **UNSOURCED**.
- **Numbers reported by Wikipedia** [WP]:
  - New teams start with "about $30,000" and 18 players (5 P, 8 batters, 5 pinch hitters).
  - Free agents cost "$5,000 to $2,980,000".
  - Each ability holds up to 15 points.
  - Each player has a maximum "(out of 90)".
  - Batters have 6 upgradable abilities and pitchers have 11.

  These figures are on Wikipedia, but the specific numbers carry no inline citation except the Weiss book (*Classic Home Video Games*, McFarland 2012), which is cited for the ability count. Treat them as secondary.
- **Corroboration from the sequel article:** "the same 15-point system introduced in the original Baseball Stars … the random points … changed from between 1 and 6 to between 1 and 5." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_Stars_2
- **Salaries:** I found no source describing recurring salaries or payroll. Money is spent only on hiring ("SIGN CONTRACT") and power-ups. [Manual] [Giglio FAQ]
  Any per-season salary mechanic is **UNSOURCED** and is probably not in the game.
- **Quick-cash exploit.** With two controllers, a player-controlled league game against the Lovely Ladies can be ended by the 10-run rule. [GF Cheats] Neoseeker also describes a power-up method of winning about 15–20 league games against the SNK Crushers: https://www.neoseeker.com/baseball-stars/cheats/nes/ (reported via search summary; I did not fetch the page directly).

## 4. Female players, and the "?" team strength

- **Hiring an individual female player is a built-in hidden option, and the manual hints at it.** Under FIELDER, the manual says: "There is also a way to decide if your player will be male or female. Can you find it?" [Manual]
- **Method.** On the Hire Player screen, keep pressing Down past the three visible options to reveal a hidden fourth option, "MALE". Press Left/Right to change it to "FEMALE".
  - Giglio FAQ: "Press Down on the d-pad until you see the word 'MALE' appear on the list. By pressing Left or Right on the d-pad, you will be able to change it from 'MALE' to 'FEMALE'." [Giglio FAQ]
  - GameFAQs cheat by SBAllen: "Keep hitting down until you come across a hidden fourth option that allows for you to choose between a male or female player." [GF Cheats]
  - Wikipedia: "a hidden feature allows players to hire female baseball players", citing Weiss 2012. [WP]
- **All-women created team (cheat).** Sources describe the input sequence differently:
  - **TCRF:** In Make Team, select and then deselect the "Veterans" strength 4 times in total, then pick any strength other than Veterans. The default team name becomes "WHAT IS A WREN?". Change it to "A BIRD.", select END, then enter the real team name. "the team will consist of women instead of men." [TCRF]
    The Japanese prompt quotes Ono no Komachi's poem from the Ogura Hyakunin Isshu: おののこまちの / しものくは → わがみよにふる / ながめせしまに. [TCRF]
  - **GameFAQs (MikeyMo82):** Down×5, then Right×7, then select "power hitters" and answer "A BIRD." [GF Cheats]
    Giglio's FAQ gives the same Down×5 / Right×7 version, credited to reader "hq99". [Giglio FAQ]
- **The "?" team strength.** The manual lists eight strengths:
  1. Consistent Hitters
  2. Power Hitters
  3. Fast Runners
  4. Good Defense
  5. Strong Pitchers
  6. Veterans
  7. Balanced Team
  8. "? (Unknown — Surprise Category)"

  [Manual]
  - TCRF notes that "?" is the default cursor position on that screen. [TCRF]
  - Wikipedia: the strength "simply favors the chosen attribute when the computer randomly generates the players". [WP]
  - What "?" actually does internally (for example, picking a random category) is **UNSOURCED**. The manual only calls it a surprise.

## 5. Other easter eggs and secrets

- **Super Team.** In Make Team, press Down, Right, Left, Down, Down, Right (GameFAQs and Giglio add Up). The team name reads "WHEN ISN'T IT?". Change it to "WHEN IT IS." to get a team with "far more power than any other team". [TCRF] [GF Cheats] [Giglio FAQ]
  The Japanese prompt and answer quote Emperor Sutoku's poem from the Ogura Hyakunin Isshu: すとくいんの / しものくは → われてもすえに / あわむとぞおもふ ("Even though we are parted, I believe we will meet again someday"). [TCRF]
- **Super Women's Team.** Combine both cheats. You are asked the wren question and then the "when" question, and the result is "a women's team with maxed out parameters". [TCRF] [Giglio FAQ]
  Giglio's sequence is Down, Right, Left, Down, Down, Right, Up, Up, Down, Up, Down, Up. [Giglio FAQ]
- **Special ending image.** Winning with a custom or hidden team shows "an additional celebratory image after the trophy scene". Default teams get only the trophy. The image differs between the JP and US versions. [TCRF] [GF Cheats]
- **Regional title differences.** If left idle on the title screen, the Japanese version shows an English version of the title, while the US version shows several baseball players. [TCRF]
- **Mercy rules.** There is a 10-run rule at the end of an inning (manual: "Called Game"). [Manual]
  Wikipedia also reports a 100-run instant-win rule, and that a game tied after 18 innings ends with no result and no stats saved. [WP]
- **Wall climb.** "Climbing the Wall … the most difficult move in the whole game". You catch a would-be home run by running into the wall and pressing Up+A/B. [Manual]
- **Proper shutdown.** Hold RESET while switching power off, to protect the battery save. [Manual]
- **Fielding AI exploit ("Confusion Spell", "Hitting a No Hitter").** Repeated pickoff throws can get the AI's fielders to vacate bases and throw the ball into center field. These are user-submitted. [GF Cheats]
- **Hidden-name homages on the default teams.** American Dreams players include Pete, Hank, Babe, Sandie, Cy, Denny and Willie (after Rose, Aaron, Ruth, Koufax, Young, McLain, Mays). Japan Robins includes "Oh" (Sadaharu Oh). [WP]

## 6. Context: development, reception, sales, legacy

- **Release.**
  - Japan: May 19, 1989, as *ベースボールスター めざせ三冠王!!* (Base Ball Star: Mezase Sankanou!!), ¥5,900, model SFX-B9. https://www.gavas.jp/products/detail.php?product_id=4929 and [TCRF]
  - North America: July 1989, model NES-B9-USA. https://www.arcade-history.com/game/54924/
  - It also ran in arcades on Nintendo's PlayChoice-10. [WP] https://www.arcade-history.com/game/54924/
- **Firsts.** Per Wikipedia, citing USA Today (1992) and Conti's *Ultimate Nintendo* (2016) [WP]:
  - The first baseball game with battery backup on any console.
  - The first NES sports game with battery backup.
  - The first NES sports game with create-a-player.
- **Development history:** I found no interview, staff account or development story. **UNSOURCED.**
  Wikipedia says *Little League Baseball: Championship Series* (1990) used the same engine. [WP]
  Some fan wikis claim the game began as an arcade title. That conflicts with the SNK 40th Anniversary Collection notes (next bullet) and is unreliable, so I did not use it.
- **SNK 40th Anniversary Collection** (Wikipedia): "Original arcade versions are included for every game except for games never released in arcades (*Baseball Stars*, *Crystalis*, and *Iron Tank*), which run the NES versions." *Baseball Stars* is marked "Exclusive to the Xbox One version" (the 26th game).
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SNK_40th_Anniversary_Collection
- **Contemporary and retrospective reception:**
  - *Computer Entertainer*, August 1989: 7.5/8. https://www.ataricompendium.com/archives/newsletters/video_game_update/computer_entertainer_aug89.pdf#page=11 (via [WP])
  - *Electronic Gaming Monthly* #100 (Nov 1997): #87 of the "100 Best Games of All Time", calling it "every bit as fun as it was eight years ago when it was the best baseball game the NES ever saw". [WP]
  - IGN Top 100 NES Games: #28. [WP]
  - *Game Informer* #100 (Aug 2001): #63 in its "Top 100 Games of All Time". http://web.archive.org/web/20220114191156/https://www.mobygames.com/game/baseball-stars/trivia
  - A Nintendo Power reader poll named it the most popular baseball game (*Milwaukee Journal Sentinel*, July 10, 1990). [WP]
  - Perfect 5 stars in Pat Conti's *Ultimate Nintendo: Guide to the NES Library* (2016). [WP]
- **Sales:** I found no unit-sales figures from any source. **UNSOURCED.**
- **Influence on EA's NHL series.** NHL producer David Littman: "Our GM mode is based on Baseball Stars". He called it "my favourite sports game of all-time" and said "Baseball Stars was the first real sports RPG."
  Kotaku AU (Owen Good, Sept 16, 2011), quoting Yahoo! Puck Daddy: http://web.archive.org/web/20110925025432/http://www.kotaku.com.au/2011/09/old-school-nes-baseball-game-inspired-ea-sports-world-class-hockey-title/
  The same article notes that Baseball Stars "had female players and an all-female team", and that NHL 12 had just announced female player creation.
- **Sequels and legacy:**
  - *Baseball Stars Professional* (Neo Geo MVS, April 26, 1990): a launch title and the first Neo Geo baseball game. It brought back the NES teams but dropped team management and team creation. Later ports include Neo Geo CD (1995) and Hamster's ACA Neo Geo (2018+). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_Stars_Professional
  - *Baseball Stars 2* (Neo Geo, 1992): 18 teams in two leagues, produced by Eikichi Kawasaki. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_Stars_2
  - *Baseball Stars II* (NES, Romstar, 1992): Nintendo Power wrote that "if you already own the original, it is not worth your money". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_Stars_2
  - *Baseball Stars* (Neo Geo Pocket) and *Baseball Stars Color* (NGPC). *Color* was re-released in *Neo Geo Pocket Color Selection Vol. 2* (2022). [WP]
  - *Super Baseball 2020* (Neo Geo) is described as a spiritual relative. [WP]
- **Retrospective framing.** SABR Baseball Gaming says its legacy is introducing "customization, season mode, and player upgrading to consoles": https://sabrbaseballgaming.com/2023/02/12/still-shining-baseball-stars-for-the-nes/
